Plot area calculator

Measure the sides of a plot and get its area in square feet, gaj, marla, guntha, cents and acres. Works for rectangles, triangles and four-sided plots that are not square.

Go round the plot: side 1 and side 2 meet at one corner, side 3 and side 4 at the opposite corner. The diagonal joins the other two corners.

How to measure a plot that is not a rectangle

  1. Measure all four sides with a tape.
  2. Measure one diagonal, corner to opposite corner. This is the step people skip, and without it the area cannot be known.
  3. The diagonal cuts the plot into two triangles. The calculator finds the area of each from its three sides and adds them.

The sums it uses

  • Rectangle: length × breadth.
  • Triangle: Heron's formula. Take half the total of the three sides (call it s). The area is the square root of s × (s − a) × (s − b) × (s − c).
  • Four sides: two triangles that share the diagonal.

Why four sides alone are not enough

A four-sided frame can be pushed flat like a folding gate without changing the length of any side. Its area shrinks as it leans. The diagonal locks the shape, which is why surveyors always take it.

Tips for a true measure

  • Keep the tape straight and level, not along a slope.
  • Measure each side twice.
  • For plots with more than four sides, split them into triangles and add the areas.
